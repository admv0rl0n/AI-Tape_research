# 2. Evidence posture

Research 2 inherits Research 1 evidence labels:

- OBSERVED — directly visible in an artifact or generated result.
- REPEATED OBSERVATION — visible across distinct lineage points or runs.
- FUNCTIONAL SURVIVAL — same useful function reappears under changed representation.
- INDEPENDENT CONVERGENCE — function emerges without obvious lexical inheritance.
- INFERRED — best current explanation of observed material.
- PREDICTED — proposed effect not established by the corpus.
- COUNTEREVIDENCE — evidence that weakens, bounds, or contextualises a claim.
- ABLATION REQUIRED — contribution plausible but not isolated.
- EXTINCT MECHANISM — historical mechanism disappeared or was pruned.
- SURVIVING FUNCTION — underlying purpose persists despite mechanism loss.

Research 2 adds practical confidence language for scars:

- VERY STRONG SCAR
- STRONG LESSON
- PLAUSIBLE LESSON
- AMBIGUOUS
- NO EVIDENCE

Important:
A source saying "verified", "validated", "high fidelity", or equivalent does not automatically establish controlled experimental validation. Contemporary enthusiasm remains evidence about the historical state, not automatic proof of the mechanism claimed.

# 3. Research 2 core discovery: many surviving principles are bounded by paired scars

Research 2 found that many durable continuity lessons are not maxima.

The project repeatedly moved through a pattern:

INSUFFICIENT
-> mechanism introduced
-> mechanism expands
-> EXCESSIVE
-> pruning / qualification
-> surviving useful region

This suggests:

> A scar shows where continuity failed.
> A paired scar reveals boundaries within which useful continuity can operate.

Important qualification:
The useful region is NOT necessarily the midpoint between the scars. It is asymmetric and moves with consequence, capability, context, evidence, and available transport.

Better formulation:

> Find the bounded region in which neither known scar is reactivated.

# 4. Paired Scar Atlas

## 4.1 Minimality <-> Bloat

Scar A:
Too little continuity preserves a lean floor but loses dynamic trade-offs, rationale, and operational richness.

Scar B:
Too much active continuity causes context degradation, forced compression, cognitive load, and hallucination risk.

Surviving lesson:

> Lean enough to load; rich enough to continue.

Mutation path:
minimal floor -> richer liferafts -> progressive recovery -> selectable depth -> multiple instruments / FULL option.

Confidence: VERY STRONG.

---

## 4.2 Rationale loss <-> Diary drift

Scar A:
The conclusion survives but the reason disappears. The successor inherits an answer without the expensive judgement that produced it.

Scar B:
Preserving every reason, discussion, and subjective detail creates diary-like continuity with poor continuation value.

Surviving lesson:

> Preserve consequential WHY.

Refinement:
Rationale earns continuity space through consequence, not historical existence.

Confidence: VERY STRONG.

---

## 4.3 Preserve everything <-> Semantic amnesia

Scar A:
Append-only history eventually exceeds practical working context.

Scar B:
Aggressive compaction removes cognitive momentum, unresolved tensions, causal history, and important distinctions.

Surviving lesson:

> Protect high-consequence structure rather than every historical representation.

This is the old compaction paradox in mature form.

Confidence: VERY STRONG FUNCTIONAL SURVIVAL.

---

## 4.4 Freedom <-> Schema cage

Scar A:
Too much freedom permits drift, hidden assumptions, shadow rules, inconsistent reconstruction, or weak activation.

Scar B:
Too much structure creates taxonomies, ceremonial metadata, compliance behaviour, and reduced intelligent judgement.

Surviving lesson:

> Hard boundaries around truth, authority, and honesty; freedom inside them.

Confidence: VERY STRONG.

---

## 4.5 Special encoding <-> Universal portability

Scar A:
Plain prose may appear token-expensive or insufficiently structured.

Scar B:
Special symbolic encodings introduce language-book / decoder / interoperability dependencies and can exclude weaker or differently configured successors.

Surviving lesson:

> Preserve recoverable semantic structure without making predecessor-specific decoding machinery mandatory.

Ordinary relational language became an unexpectedly strong baseline.

Confidence: VERY STRONG.

---

## 4.6 Tool power <-> Tool dependency

Scar A:
External tools can improve processing, checking, storage, analysis, or compression.

Scar B:
If successor recovery depends on the same tool being present, continuity becomes brittle.

Surviving lesson:

> Tools are ephemeral; recovered state and recoverable meaning are canonical.

Confidence: STRONG FUNCTIONAL CONVERGENCE.

---

## 4.7 Working posture <-> Authority inflation

Scar A:
State can be present while the successor lacks orientation about what matters and how to proceed.

Scar B:
Rich posture can acquire unintended authority, particularly when newer or more vivid material outweighs an older protected floor.

Surviving lesson:

> Recover useful posture without allowing posture to outrank its authority.

Confidence: STRONG.

---

## 4.8 Activation <-> Semantic hijack

Scar A:
Correct information can be present but passive, poorly weighted, or functionally ignored.

Scar B:
Highly salient, vivid, dramatic, or architecturally framed language can dominate judgement beyond its evidential warrant.

Surviving lesson:

> Make important meaning salient enough to act on without allowing linguistic salience to substitute for evidence.

Useful refinement of semantic gravity:

> Useful gravity attracts appropriate judgement.
> Bad gravity distorts weighting.

Confidence: STRONG.

---

## 4.9 Confidence <-> Completeness

Scar A:
A successor may lack enough orientation to act.

Scar B:
A coherent handoff can feel complete while granular history, implementation, assets, or rationale remain absent.

Surviving lesson:

> Bounded confidence: sufficiently oriented to act does not mean possessing everything.

Confidence: STRONG.

---

## 4.10 Forgotten rejection <-> Frozen dogma

Scar A:
If rejection rationale disappears, a failed option looks viable again and expensive mistakes are repeated.

Scar B:
If historical rejection becomes permanent prohibition, changing conditions cannot legitimately reopen the path.

Surviving lesson:

> Preserve why the path was closed, not merely that it was closed.

The first pole is strongly observed. The frozen-dogma pole is strongly inferred from authority and successor-independence scars.

Confidence: STRONG FIRST POLE / PLAUSIBLE EQUILIBRIUM.

---

## 4.11 Weak guidance <-> Protocol machinery

Scar A:
Without useful bearings, capable intelligence wastes effort reconstructing avoidable context or drifts into assumptions.

Scar B:
Protocol machinery expands until it attempts to implement intelligence through drivers, orchestration engines, operating-system metaphors, rigid schemas, or pseudo-control flow.

Surviving lesson:

> Give capable intelligence high-value bearings, evidence, scars, and boundaries; let intelligence perform the reconstruction.

Confidence: VERY STRONG FUNCTIONAL TRAJECTORY.

---

## 4.12 Security floor <-> Attention starvation by governance

Scar A:
Weakly represented authority permits experimental/posture material to become de facto law.

Scar B:
Large governance/meta-specification can displace the project reality it was meant to protect.

Surviving lesson:

> The floor must be small, active, unmistakable, and insufficiently large to become the work itself.

Confidence: VERY STRONG.

---

## 4.13 Orientation <-> Imitation

Scar A:
No predecessor orientation forces unnecessary reconstruction.

Scar B:
Too much predecessor posture encourages copying framework, voice, conclusions, or identity rather than independent continuation.

Surviving lesson:

> Recover project reality, constraints, expensive understanding, live questions, and agency; do not require predecessor imitation.

Confidence: STRONG FUNCTIONAL TRAJECTORY.

---

## 4.14 Explicit status <-> Metadata burden

Scar A:
Failure to preserve status collapses simulated into verified, experimental into authoritative, missing into false, and provisional into committed.

Scar B:
Treating every statement as a multi-axis status record recreates schema bloat.

Surviving lesson:

> Make status explicit where a status error would materially change belief, authority, action, safety, or continuation. Otherwise use ordinary language.

Confidence: STRONG.

---

## 4.15 Human authority <-> Human courier bottleneck

Scar A:
AI optimisation can shrink, overbuild, or reinterpret against the human's purpose.

Scar B:
A system requiring the human to manually carry, explain, validate, and repair each handoff makes continuity depend on low human IOPS.

Surviving lesson:

Human:
- sets purpose;
- controls acceptable loss;
- challenges overreach;
- invokes full depth where needed;
- arbitrates consequential ambiguity.

AI/artifact:
- performs routine preservation;
- declares gaps;
- carries evidence/rationale;
- reconstructs intelligently.

Confidence: STRONG.

---

## 4.16 Weak-model accessibility <-> Capability suppression

Scar A:
A representation inaccessible to weaker receivers undermines graceful portability.

Scar B:
Designing all continuity around the weakest receiver can constrain stronger systems to impoverished structures and over-scaffolding.

Surviving lesson:

> Keep a recoverable base; do not make the base the capability ceiling.

Confidence: STRONG FUNCTIONAL TRAJECTORY.

---

## 4.17 Optionality <-> Invisibility

Scar A:
Mandatory mechanisms cause ceremonial output and constraint.

Scar B:
Optional mechanisms can become so soft that they are never activated even when useful.

Surviving lesson:

> Optional means use when it materially helps, not decorative or irrelevant.

Confidence: PLAUSIBLE.

---

## 4.18 Passive documentation <-> Premature action

Scar A:
A handoff that only documents state restores no practical agency.

Scar B:
A successor may act past its evidence, verification, or dependency state.

Surviving lesson:

> Restore agency, but bind action depth to evidence, reversibility, dependency, lock-in, and risk.

Concrete historical shape:
DO NOW / INVESTIGATE / DEFER / DO NOT YET.

Confidence: VERY STRONG EXEMPLAR.

# 5. Research 2 key conceptual shift: preserving decision surfaces

Repeated scar analysis suggests that merely preserving what was decided is often less useful than preserving enough of the judgement landscape to allow the decision to be understood and, where conditions change, reconsidered.

Provisional term:

DECISION SURFACE

A decision surface may include:
- available options;
- forces supporting or opposing them;
- constraints;
- thresholds;
- evidence;
- uncertainties;
- epistemic/authority status;
- costs/trade-offs;
- rejected alternatives;
- resulting action consequences.

Critical discipline:

> Decision Surface is an explanatory research model, not a proposed Tape schema.

---

# 6. Strong exemplar: Project Aphelion

The recovered Aphelion lineage preserves a real decision landscape rather than just a winning answer.

Examples captured in the historical handoff include:
- passive optical filtering rejected because group delay / phase-margin behaviour violated the governing floor;
- a second-order approach offered better jitter but failed the phase-margin threshold;
- a first-order approach yielded somewhat poorer jitter but retained adequate phase margin and therefore became the working design;
- 16-bit fixed-point quantisation was rejected due to limit-cycle oscillation;
- 24-bit remained the current simulated baseline;
- physical verification remained outstanding.

Why this matters:

The successor does not merely inherit:

> Use first-order 24-bit filtering.

It inherits enough geometry to understand:
- why the winner won;
- what the winner sacrificed;
- which threshold excluded alternatives;
- which evidence remains provisional;
- what new evidence could reopen a rejected option.

Evidence status:
DECISION-SURFACE PRESERVATION — OBSERVED EXEMPLAR.

# 7. Results are not always the most valuable continuity object

A verdict-only handoff says:

> Use X.

A judgement-bearing handoff may say:

> X was selected because Y remained above threshold Z despite disadvantage Q; alternative A failed under condition B; current result remains simulated pending C.

The second allows the successor to recover the selected answer AND the conditions under which the answer remains sensible.

Research 2 inference:

> Preserve enough of the basis for the answer that a capable successor can reassess it when conditions change.

# 8. Three kinds of inherited decision

Research 2 tentatively distinguishes:

## Open decision
Evidence incomplete. The successor should decide.

## Working decision
Current best position. Reason, evidence, uncertainty, and relevant constraints remain available. Can change with evidence.

## Protected decision
An expensive lesson has stabilised sufficiently that it should not be casually reopened, though evidence can still justify revision.

Important:
"Protected" does not mean metaphysically unchangeable.
It means the cost/evidential threshold for reopening is relatively high.

# 9. Force becomes concrete under the decision-surface model

The old handle "Force" becomes less mysterious in Research 2.

Possible operation:

> Which parts of the inherited decision surface remain decision-relevant now?

Types of force may include:
- causal;
- epistemic;
- normative;
- historical;
- authoritative.

Thus:

Historical importance != current force.

Word-specific leverage of "Force" remains UNPROVEN.
The function is useful regardless of the handle.

# 10. Survives gains a stronger interpretation

Weak interpretation:

> Does concept X still appear in the artifacts?

Stronger interpretation:

> Do the conditions which made judgement X appropriate continue to hold?

Research 2 therefore prefers constraint/function survival over lexical survival.

# 11. Evolution, Force, Tension, Trajectory, Understanding Effect

Research 2 reveals a possible functional sequence among several late Tape dimensions:

RATIONALE
Why did the judgement arise?

TENSION
What competing valid realities shaped it?

EVOLUTION
How did the judgement landscape change historically?

UNDERSTANDING EFFECT
What learning materially changed the decision surface?

FORCE
Which inherited elements still exert decision-relevant influence now?

TRAJECTORY
As those forces change, where does the viable region appear to be moving?

This is an explanatory mapping, not proof that the dimensions are reducible to one another.

---

# 12. Relational operators acquire functional roles

Research 2 increasingly supports the importance of operations rather than sacred words.

BECAUSE
Carries supporting cause / winning rationale.

DESPITE
Carries the countervailing force or cost that did not win but remained real.

UNTIL
Carries temporal/conditional validity boundary.

UNLESS
Carries reopening/reconsideration boundary.

DEPENDS ON
Carries structural dependency.

DOES NOT IMPLY
Carries epistemic/distinction boundary.

SUPERSEDES + WHY
Carries meaningful evolution rather than chronology alone.

Example structural compression:

X selected because Y despite Z until Q.

This can encode state, rationale, counterforce, and validity condition using ordinary language.

Research 2 does NOT recommend unnatural formulaic prose.
The finding is that ordinary relational operators can carry high judgement density without decoder dependencies.

---

# 13. Ordinary relational language as semantic compression

Earlier project eras searched for compression via symbolic dictionaries, encoded structures, machine-native languages, and special mechanisms.

Research 2 suggests a simpler mechanism may already exist in natural language:

- because;
- despite;
- until;
- unless;
- depends on;
- does not imply;
- constrained by;
- supports;
- contradicts.

These compact operators substantially constrain reconstruction while remaining human-readable, model-readable, portable, and decoder-free.

Research 2 hypothesis:

> Some of the best semantic compression may come from preserving high-value relationships rather than compressing vocabulary.

Status: STRONG INFERENCE, NOT UNIVERSALLY PROVEN.

# 14. Thresholds are high-density continuity objects

A threshold may carry:
- measurement;
- acceptable region;
- decision boundary;
- future evaluation criterion.

In Aphelion, a phase-margin threshold explains why one alternative failed while a less attractive alternative survived.

Research 2 distinguishes:

CONSTRAINT
Shapes possible solution space.

THRESHOLD
Marks a decision boundary within that space.

Thresholds can preserve counterfactual recoverability very efficiently.

# 15. Counterfactual recoverability

A strong decision surface permits a successor to answer:

> What would need to change for the inherited judgement to change legitimately?

This is more important than adding an explicit `reconsider_if` field everywhere.

If the inherited reason and governing condition are clear, the successor can often infer the valid counterfactual.

Candidate property:

COUNTERFACTUAL RECOVERABILITY

Status: STRONG INFERENCE FROM DECISION-SURFACE EXEMPLARS.

---

# 16. Re-derivation defence without dogma

Research 2's strongest answer to the negative-knowledge problem is:

Do NOT preserve:

> Never use X.

Prefer preserving enough reality to recover:

> X was rejected because A violated B under conditions C.

Then the successor can ask:
- Does B still apply?
- Does C still hold?
- Has A materially changed?

This protects against casual repetition while permitting legitimate revision.

Research 2 formulation:

> Preserve the reason strongly enough to defend against bad re-derivation, but conditionally enough to avoid intellectual lock-in.

# 17. Successor freedom is evidence-dependent

Weak freedom:

> Challenge inherited decisions.

Without evidence this can mean guess.

Strong freedom:

> The successor receives enough evidence, constraints, tension, uncertainty, and rationale to disagree intelligently.

Research 2 refinement:

> Successor independence requires inherited evidence, not merely permission.

# 18. Scars refined

Research 2 now defines a scar more tightly:

> A scar is a past outcome whose loss would make a successor materially more likely to repeat a harmful, wasteful, misleading, or already-resolved path.

A failure alone is not necessarily a scar.

A scar is consequential feedback that altered future judgement.

This produces the loop:

DECISION
-> ACTION
-> OBSERVED CONSEQUENCE
-> FEEDBACK
-> SCAR / LEARNING
-> DECISION SURFACE CHANGES
-> NEW DECISION

This may be the useful operation behind the older candidate handle "Feedback".

---

# 19. Scar half-lives

Not all scars should exert permanent force.

Research 2 tentatively distinguishes:

DURABLE SCAR
General epistemic or reasoning failure with broad applicability.
Example class: do not invent absent evidence.

CONDITIONAL SCAR
Valid while its governing conditions remain present.
Example class: a particular implementation failed under a particular architecture/constraint.

HISTORICAL SCAR
Explains evolution but may no longer constrain current action directly.
Example class: context-window limits which motivated an older transport compromise.

Purpose:
Prevent the Scar Atlas from becoming a permanent blacklist of history.

# 20. Judgement state

Research 2 introduces a cautious descriptive term:

JUDGEMENT STATE

Project state says:

> Where are things?

Judgement state says:

> Why does this region of the solution space currently look preferable, dangerous, uncertain, closed, or promising?

It may be composed from:
- active forces;
- constraints;
- thresholds;
- trade-offs;
- scars;
- evidence;
- uncertainties;
- authority;
- available alternatives.

This is NOT a proposed serialisation schema.
It is an explanation of what can be lost when a handoff preserves answers but not enough of the judgement landscape.

# 28. Counterevidence still required

The most attractive Research 2 idea is Decision Surface preservation.

Therefore the next evidential obligation is NOT to elaborate it.

Look for historical cases where:
- rich rationale harmed continuation;
- preserving tensions preserved stale conflict that should have died;
- rejected alternatives created distraction rather than defence;
- simple verdicts were sufficient and richer context was needless baggage;
- thresholds were obsolete but continued to exert inappropriate force;
- preserving too much judgement biased successors toward predecessor thinking;
- clear relational language still failed because activation/authority was wrong.

If these exist, they define the boundary of Decision Surface usefulness.
