## 0. Purpose

Preserve the hostile counterexample pass that followed Research 2.

Research 3 does not introduce a new continuity architecture. It tests the explanatory theories emerging from Research 2 against historical cases where less continuity was sufficient, richer continuity became harmful, old context became stale, alternatives created noise, or predecessor judgement constrained successor intelligence.

This tape records what survived that attack.

It is NOT:
- a new AI Tape specification;
- an amendment to T4A, T4A_FULL, or the Continuity Guide;
- a Decision Surface schema;
- a recommendation to preserve all rationale;
- a new testing programme;
- a claim that every decision warrants deep continuity.

---

# 1. Incoming Research 2 position

Research 2's strongest archaeological finding was:

> Durable continuity lessons often emerge from paired failures rather than single successes.

Its most attractive explanatory theory was:

> Preserve enough of the decision surface that a capable successor can know why the decision won, what opposed it, whether those forces still apply, and what would justify choosing differently.

External hostile review accepted Paired Scars more strongly than Decision Surface and warned that Research 2 was drifting from archaeology toward elegant theory.

The required next move was not expansion.

It was counterexample search.

---

# 2. Hostile research questions

Research 3 searched the lineage for cases where:

1. Rich rationale increased load or bias without improving continuation.
2. A simple verdict was sufficient.
3. Preserving alternatives created distraction or reopened settled work.
4. Old thresholds, constraints, or scars continued exerting force after applicability weakened.
5. Predecessor judgement crowded out successor intelligence.
6. Rich relational framing produced pseudo-depth rather than evidence-backed understanding.

The research posture remained retrospective. No new manufactured testing was introduced.

---

# 3. Main result

The hostile pass weakens Decision Surface as a universal continuity object.

It strengthens Paired Scars.

Revised conclusion:

> Decision Surface is not a continuity requirement. It is an escalation option for consequential judgement that cannot safely or cheaply be reconstructed.

This is a meaningful demotion.

Decision Surface remains useful as a broad explanatory metaphor.
It does not survive as a general prescription.

---

# 4. Counterexample: sometimes the verdict is enough

Historical bounded checkpoints successfully restored:
- authority posture;
- identity honesty;
- active experiment/status;
- critical gaps;
- one safe next action;
- zero-tolerance boundaries against false compliance or simulation-to-reality laundering.

They did not carry complete historical rationale.

For bounded operational purposes, that could be sufficient.

A successor may need only:

- the current verdict;
- material status;
- explicit known gaps;
- the necessary action boundary.

It may not need:

- the complete alternative landscape;
- every previous tension;
- all rejected paths;
- the full evolution of the judgement.

Research 3 lesson:

> Depth should be judged against purpose, not against maximum possible preservation.

---

# 5. Counterexample: rationale can become active noise

The archive had already identified diary drift.

Technical rationale can expand into:
- subjective narrative;
- historical deliberation with no current consequence;
- predecessor interpretation more vivid than source evidence;
- stale tensions;
- abandoned nuance;
- attention burden.

Therefore:

> Preserve why the decision remains consequential.
> Do not preserve every thought involved in reaching it.

Rich explanation is not automatically rich continuity.

---

# 6. Counterexample: alternatives can create branching pressure

Preserved alternatives may provide useful negative knowledge.

They may also:
- invite reopening of settled work;
- generate option overload;
- increase branching;
- create architecture inflation;
- attract attention merely because they are interesting;
- turn evidence into an implementation backlog.

Research 3 admission rule for alternatives:

A rejected option earns continued visibility when at least one is true:
1. its rejection reason remains active;
2. changed conditions could plausibly revive it;
3. losing it creates meaningful risk of repeated waste, harm, or false re-derivation.

Otherwise, pruning or archive-only retention is appropriate.

---

# 7. Counterexample: historical constraints can become stale authority

Preservation alone does not establish present applicability.

A threshold, floor, prohibition, or rejection condition can remain perfectly represented after the world that justified it has changed.

Therefore every inherited constraint carries an implicit hostile question:

> Does the condition which gave this constraint authority still apply?

Historical importance is not current force.

This especially affects:
- numeric thresholds;
- transport assumptions;
- model capability assumptions;
- context-window limits;
- old architecture restrictions;
- previously necessary process controls.

Evidence posture:
The corpus strongly supports authority/weighting drift as a general failure. A perfectly isolated stale numeric-threshold case was not established. This remains a strong inference rather than a fully observed standalone scar.

---

# 8. Counterexample: predecessor judgement can constrain successor intelligence

Rich recovered judgement can become:
- precedent the successor feels obliged to follow;
- framing lock-in;
- an attention sink;
- accidental authority;
- a reproduction target;
- a barrier to discovering a better representation or solution.

This restates an old project scar in a new setting:

> The AI-generated specification can become a rigid wall of bureaucracy.

Research 3 lesson:

> Transfer evidence for judgement, not ownership of judgement.

A successor should inherit enough reality to disagree intelligently, but not so much predecessor framing that only one way of thinking remains visible.

---

# 9. Counterexample: constitutional recovery may be the correct target

Some handoffs were designed to recover:
- floor authority;
- experimental status;
- prose fallback;
- declared missing assets;
- safe working posture.

They explicitly did not carry:
- deep history bundles;
- granular implementation;
- full workspace contents;
- every decision surface.

For the task of restoring boundaries and awaiting further material, this was appropriate.

Research 3 distinguishes:

## Constitutional recovery
Authority, boundaries, status, honesty controls.

## Operational recovery
Current assets, actions, dependencies, constraints.

## Judgement recovery
Why, rejected alternatives, trade-offs, threshold logic.

## Trajectory recovery
What changed and where the work appears to be moving.

Different handoffs may legitimately target different layers.

---

# 10. Known absence strengthens as safe compression

Research 3 finds an additional role for known absence.

A handoff can safely omit deep history when it preserves:

- what is absent;
- why the absence matters;
- where the source can be retrieved;
- what action remains safe without it.

Therefore:

> A declared gap can be safer than an inadequately compressed explanation.

Known absence is both continuity content and a compression mechanism.

---

# 11. New failure class: pseudo-depth

Pseudo-depth occurs when sophisticated explanatory language substitutes for missing evidence.

Pattern:

missing source reality
+
rich abstract explanation
+
confident semantic language
=
false impression of recovered judgement

Examples of risky framing include unsupported claims about:
- durable trajectory;
- active force;
- complete decision landscapes;
- recovered rationale;
- universal semantic structure.

Research 3 principle:

> Honest compactness is better than unsupported depth.

A bounded checkpoint that declares its gaps is preferable to a polished reconstruction which hides them.

---

# 12. Decision Surface becomes Decision Basis

The hostile review asked whether Decision Surface predicted or preserved anything beyond:

- rationale;
- constraints;
- alternatives;
- uncertainty;
- evidence.

Research 3 concludes that the term Decision Surface encourages excessive geometry and over-unification.

A smaller practical description is:

## Decision Basis

> The consequential evidence, constraints, trade-offs, uncertainty, and relationships needed to understand or reassess a judgement.

Advantages over Decision Surface:
- less architecture-like;
- does not imply complete mapping of the solution space;
- naturally supports partial preservation;
- does not demand irrelevant alternatives;
- works for simple verdict-plus-reason cases;
- scales without requiring schema.

Status:

Decision Surface:
- retain as broad research metaphor;
- do not prescribe;
- do not implement as schema.

Decision Basis:
- narrower practical description;
- promising;
- still not a required field set.

# 13. The preservation router

Research 3 derives a small judgement aid from the scars.

It is NOT a Tape procedure.
It is an analytical guide for deciding how much continuity a piece of material deserves.

## Question 1
Does this material affect current belief, authority, judgement, or action?

- No: archive or omit.
- Yes: continue.

## Question 2
Would losing its basis make incorrect re-derivation costly, dangerous, misleading, or likely?

- No: verdict/status may suffice.
- Yes: preserve more basis.

## Question 3
Is the source evidence stable and cheaply recoverable?

- Yes: pointer + declared gap may suffice.
- No: preserve essential evidence and relationships.

## Question 4
Could conditions legitimately change?

- No or negligible: consequence/action boundary may suffice.
- Yes: preserve enough basis for reassessment.

## Question 5
Would richer predecessor context anchor or distract the successor?

- Yes: keep it available rather than active.
- No: foreground if materially useful.

Purpose:
Consequence-sensitive, role-aware preservation without fixed schema.

---

# 14. Re-derivation cost becomes the primary control variable

Research 3 suggests that Decision Basis depth should be proportional not merely to importance, but to the expected damage of incorrect re-derivation.

Preserve more when re-derivation would be:
- expensive;
- dangerous;
- highly uncertain;
- misleading;
- dependent on unavailable evidence;
- likely to repeat a known failure;
- likely to acquire false authority.

Preserve less when the judgement is:
- routine;
- low consequence;
- easy to verify;
- cheap to reconstruct;
- unlikely to be revisited;
- well supported by stable accessible sources.

Research 3 formulation:

> Preserve judgement basis in proportion to the expected damage of incorrect re-derivation.

This is currently the smallest practical survivor of the broader Decision Surface idea.

---

# 15. Source accessibility changes preservation burden

Continuity depth depends on source recoverability.

High re-derivation cost + poor source accessibility:
- preserve richer basis.

Low re-derivation cost + strong source accessibility:
- verdict + reference + declared gap may suffice.

This prevents travelling artifacts from carrying evidence that can be cheaply recovered while also preventing critical inaccessible evidence from disappearing behind a pointer.

---

# 16. Active / Available / Archived

Research 2 introduced scar half-lives.
Research 3 demotes that taxonomy in favour of current role.

## Active
Currently affects judgement, authority, belief, or action.

## Available
Retrievable if the judgement is revisited, but not foregrounded now.

## Archived
Retained for audit, archaeology, or historical explanation only.

Advantages:
- a scar can change role as conditions change;
- an old threshold can move from active constraint to archived explanation;
- rejected alternatives can remain available without cluttering active continuity;
- deep reasoning can be retained without anchoring routine continuation.

Research 3 conclusion:

> Current role may matter more than intrinsic scar age.

---

# 17. Counterfactual Recoverability survives, but is scoped

The external reviewer identified this question as a possible hidden gem:

> What would need to change for the inherited judgement to change legitimately?

Research 3 retains it for:
- consequential decisions;
- revisitable judgements;
- condition-sensitive rejections;
- expensive re-derivation;
- contexts where dogma and amnesia are both material risks.

It should not be required for:
- routine facts;
- cheap decisions;
- readily reproducible conclusions;
- settled low-risk state;
- material whose source evidence is easily retrieved.

Status:
PROMISING, USEFUL, CONDITIONALLY APPLICABLE.

---

# 18. Full, Lite, and Archive are purpose classes

Research 3 reframes depth profiles.

## Lite
Optimised for bounded orientation, truth, gaps, and immediate safe action.

## Full
Optimised for deeper judgement inheritance, expensive understanding, scars, tension, and successor reassessment.

## Archive
Optimised for audit, archaeology, provenance, and retrospective evidence.

These are not competing truth levels.

A Lite artifact is not inferior when Lite is the task.
A Full artifact is not superior when its depth becomes noise.
An Archive should not be forced into the active context merely because it exists.

Research 3 principle:

> Depth is evaluated against purpose, not maximum preservation.

## Useful region

> Consequence-sensitive, role-aware preservation of only the judgement basis whose loss would materially impair continuation.

# 20. Revised central proposition

Research 2 proposition:

> Preserve enough of the decision surface that a capable successor can know why the decision won, what opposed it, whether those forces still apply, and what would justify choosing differently.

Research 3 revision:

> For consequential judgement that cannot be safely or cheaply reconstructed, preserve enough of its basis to explain the current verdict, maintain material boundaries, and permit legitimate reassessment. Keep non-current depth available rather than automatically active.

This is less elegant.
It is also less likely to become machinery.

# 29. Final handover

Research 3 attacked Research 2's most attractive theory and forced it to contract.

The broad idea survived only conditionally.

The lineage does support preserving rich judgement context where:
- the decision is consequential;
- the basis is difficult to reconstruct;
- source evidence is inaccessible;
- conditions may change;
- bad re-derivation is costly;
- the successor may legitimately need to reassess.

The lineage does not support carrying this depth everywhere.

For routine or low-risk continuation, a compact verdict with material status, action boundary, and declared gaps may be entirely sufficient.

The resulting Research 3 position is:

> The best continuity does not preserve the maximum possible past. It preserves the minimum sufficient reality needed for the successor's actual purpose, while keeping deeper judgement available when losing it would materially impair intelligent continuation.

Stop theory expansion here.
